How it scores

  • G-1 Content match ≥ 55% of key terms, numbers, dates, owner and speaker all agree.
  • P-1 Content matches, but a field disagrees: number, date, owner or speaker.
  • P-2 Weak match, 30–55%: possibly a paraphrase the lexical match missed.
  • U-1 No line above 30%: nothing in the transcript supports the claim.
  • R-1 A commitment in the transcript (“I’ll…”, “we’ll…”, “let’s…”) with no matching action item.
  • A-1 / A-2 Quote attributed to the wrong speaker, or to a speaker under 10% talk share.

Lexical + rules-based matching. No LLM anywhere in this demo.

Why this exists. Circleback’s Oct 20 post, “How Circleback Writes Meeting Notes and How to Check Them”, tells users to verify notes by hand in four manual steps, and warns to “check the speaker names before you forward an attributed quote or action item.” Groundcheck automates that check.

About this demo & its limits

The three meetings are fictional, written so every check has something real to find and something real to pass. Matching is lexical and rules-based: token overlap plus checks on numbers, dates, owners and speakers. Paraphrases can slip past it, so a true claim worded very differently may read PARTIAL, and a flag means “check this,” not “wrong.” It cannot judge meaning, tone, or whether a decision was wise. It needs the transcript: meetings whose transcripts were deleted under retention settings cannot be audited. A production version would add an LLM judge with a labeled eval set; this is the deterministic floor that eval set would be built on.
